\section{Randomised Partitions and Aggregation}
\label{sec:aggregation}

Every estimator of Sections~\ref{sec:rom}--\ref{sec:mor} and every test of Section~\ref{s:inference} begins by cutting the sample into $k$ blocks, and the proofs require only that the blocks be disjoint, of equal size, and fixed in advance of the data. In an application, samples are commonly ordered, whether by date, region, or cohort, so blocks cut from the file as it stands would differ for reasons unrelated to sampling noise. Permuting the rows uniformly at random before cutting removes this dependence on the file order, at the cost of making the estimate a function of the seed. We show that the guarantees survive the randomisation, that they say little about a fixed sample under a varying seed, and that aggregating over permutations removes the seed dependence for a factor two in $\delta$.

\begin{definition}[Randomised and aggregated estimators]
\label{def:randomised}
    Let $S = (Y_i,X_i,Z_i)_{i=1}^{n}$ be the sample and $S^{(\pi)} = (Y_{\pi(i)},X_{\pi(i)},Z_{\pi(i)})_{i=1}^{n}$ its reordering by a permutation $\pi$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$. Let $\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi)})$ be any of the block-based estimators of Sections~\ref{sec:rom}--\ref{sec:mor} computed on $S^{(\pi)}$, the blocks being the consecutive runs of $m = \lfloor n/k\rfloor$ positions, and let $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_B$ be drawn independently and uniformly at random, independently of $S$. The randomised estimator is $\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_1)})$ and the aggregated estimator is $\widetilde{\beta}_B = \med(\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_1)}),\dots,\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_B)}))$. Different aggregation methods can be used; we utilise the median. Write $\widetilde{\beta}_\infty$ for the median of the $n!$ values $\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})$, one for each permutation $p$; the idealised aggregate obtained when every permutation is used.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Transfer to the randomised estimator]
\label{prop:transfer}
    Assume \ref{ass:exog}--\ref{ass:moments} and let $\hat{\beta}$ be the Ratio-of-Medians or Median-of-Ratios estimator, with $k$, $m$ and the instrument strength condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:rom} or Theorem~\ref{thm:mor} in force, and let $t = t(n,k,\delta)$ be the deviation appearing there, so that $\Prob_S\!\left[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S)-\beta} > t\right] \leq \delta$. Then
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:transfer}
        \Prob_{S,\pi}\!\left[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi)}) - \beta} > t\right] \leq \delta.
    \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Fix a permutation $p$. Since $S$ is i.i.d.\ it is exchangeable, so $S^{(p)}$ has the same distribution as $S$, and the hypothesis applied to that common distribution gives
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:fixed_perm}
        \Prob_S\!\left[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})-\beta} > t\right] \leq \delta .
    \end{equation}
    As $\pi$ is uniform on the $n!$ permutations and independent of $S$, conditioning on its value gives $\Prob_{S,\pi}\!\left[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi)})-\beta} > t\!\right] = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{p} \Prob_S\!\left[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})-\beta} > t\!\right] \leq \delta$.
\end{proof}

Randomising the blocking therefore costs nothing; the randomised estimator inherits the guarantee of a fixed partition, and the same argument applies to the size bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:mom_ar_size}. What the argument needs is that $\pi$ be drawn independently of the data. A seed chosen after looking at $S$, such as the one giving the most favourable estimate, changes the distribution of the reordered sample, and \eqref{eq:transfer} may then fail. Note also that \eqref{eq:transfer} averages over the sample and the seed together. An applied researcher is in a different position, holding the sample fixed and varying only the seed.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional failure fraction]
\label{prop:conditional}
    In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, let
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:failure_fraction}
        W(S) = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{p} \mathbf{1}\!\left\{\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})-\beta} > t\right\}
    \end{equation}
    be the fraction of permutations that fail on the sample $S$. Then $\E_S[W(S)] \leq \delta$ and $\Prob_S[W(S) \geq \lambda] \leq \delta/\lambda$ for every $\lambda \in (0,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Taking expectations over $S$ in \eqref{eq:failure_fraction} and exchanging the finite sum with the expectation, $\E_S[W(S)] = \frac{1}{n!}\sum_{p} \Prob_S[\abs{\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})-\beta} > t] \leq \delta$, each term being bounded by \eqref{eq:fixed_perm}. Since $W(S) \geq 0$, Markov's inequality gives $\Prob_S[W(S) \geq \lambda] \leq \E_S[W(S)]/\lambda \leq \delta/\lambda$.
\end{proof}

At $\lambda = 1/2$ this says only that, outside a $2\delta$ fraction of samples, fewer than half of the permutations fail. It says nothing about how far apart the resulting estimates are, which is instead measured empirically in Section~\ref{sss:ps}.

\begin{lemma}[Majority in an interval]
\label{lem:majority}
    Let $x_1,\dots,x_N \in \R$ and let $I \subseteq \R$ be an interval containing more than $N/2$ of the $x_j$. Then every median of $x_1,\dots,x_N$ lies in $I$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Let $a$ be the number of $x_j$ lying below $I$ and $c$ the number lying above, so that $a + c < N/2$. Sorting the values as $x_{(1)} \leq \dots \leq x_{(N)}$, those in $I$ occupy the positions $a+1,\dots,N-c$. From $a < N/2$ we get $a + 1 \leq \lceil N/2 \rceil$, and from $c < N/2$ we get $N - c \geq \lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1$, so the two central values $x_{(\lceil N/2 \rceil)}$ and $x_{(\lfloor N/2 \rfloor + 1)}$ both lie in $I$. Every median lies between them, and $I$ is an interval, so it lies in $I$ as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Concentration of the aggregated estimator]
\label{thm:aggregation}
    In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}, the aggregated estimator of Definition~\ref{def:randomised} satisfies, for every $B \geq 1$ and every $\lambda \in (0,1/2)$,
    \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:agg_bound}
        \Prob_S\!\left[\abs{\widetilde{\beta}_\infty - \beta} > t\right] \leq 2\delta ,
        \qquad
        \Prob\!\left[\abs{\widetilde{\beta}_B - \beta} > t\right] \leq \frac{\delta}{\lambda} + e^{-2B(1/2-\lambda)^2}.
    \end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    \noindent\textbf{Step 1 (All permutations).} Write $I = [\beta-t,\beta+t]$, so that a permutation $p$ fails precisely when $\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)}) \notin I$. If $W(S) < 1/2$ then more than half of the $n!$ values $\hat{\beta}(S^{(p)})$ lie in $I$, so $\widetilde{\beta}_\infty \in I$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:majority}. Hence $\Prob_S[\abs{\widetilde{\beta}_\infty-\beta} > t] \leq \Prob_S[W(S) \geq 1/2] \leq 2\delta$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional} at $\lambda = 1/2$.

    \medskip\noindent\textbf{Step 2 (Finitely many permutations).} Fix the sample $S$ and set $\zeta_b = \mathbf{1}\{\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_b)}) \notin I\}$. As each $\pi_b$ is uniform and independent of $S$, the probability that it fails is exactly $W(S)$, so $\zeta_1,\dots,\zeta_B$ are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli with mean $W(S)$. If fewer than $B/2$ of them equal one, more than half of the values $\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_b)})$ lie in $I$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:majority} gives $\widetilde{\beta}_B \in I$. Whenever $W(S) \leq \lambda$, the aggregate can therefore fail only if $\sum_b \zeta_b$ exceeds its mean by at least $B(1/2-\lambda)$, so Hoeffding's inequality gives
    \[
        \Prob\!\left[\abs{\widetilde{\beta}_B-\beta} > t \;\middle|\; S\right]
        \leq \Prob\!\left[\sum\nolimits_{b} \zeta_b \geq B/2 \;\middle|\; S\right]
        \leq e^{-2B(1/2-\lambda)^2}.
    \]
    Averaging over $S$ now gives the second bound: the display applies to every sample with $W(S) \leq \lambda$, and the remaining samples occur with probability at most $\delta/\lambda$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The price, in blocks]
\label{rem:agg_price}
    The two bounds in \eqref{eq:agg_bound} price the aggregation differently. For the idealised aggregate $\widetilde{\beta}_\infty$, running the underlying estimator at $\delta/2$ recovers the original bound at level $\delta$; for the Median-of-Ratios estimator this replaces $k = \lceil 8\ln(1/\delta)\rceil$ by $k = \lceil 8\ln(2/\delta)\rceil$, the block count the Ratio-of-Medians estimator already pays for its union bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:rom}. For finite $B$ the leading term is $\delta/\lambda$ rather than $2\delta$, so a choice of $\lambda$ must be fixed: at $\lambda = 1/3$, running the underlying estimator at $\delta/3$, i.e.\ $k = \lceil 8\ln(3/\delta)\rceil$, gives $\delta/\lambda + e^{-2B(1/2-\lambda)^2} \le \delta + e^{-B/18}$, and the exponential term is negligible, contributing at most $2\cdot 10^{-5}$ at $B = 200$. In exchange, the estimate no longer depends on a seed.
\end{remark}


\begin{remark}[Why the median and not the mean]
\label{rem:agg_mean}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregation} uses only that a majority of permutations succeed; the failing minority may be arbitrarily bad without moving the median outside $I$. Averaging does not have the same guarantee, since one extreme value moves the mean by an arbitrary amount. Since the estimators are ratios, and a partition placing an unusually small value in a denominator produces an arbitrarily large estimate, which a mean would pass on.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The permutation distribution is not a sampling distribution]
\label{rem:agg_not_sampling}
    With $S$ held fixed the draws $\hat{\beta}(S^{(\pi_b)})$ are i.i.d., so their spread shrinks as $B$ grows; it measures the computational randomness of the blocking, not uncertainty about $\beta$. An interval formed from their quantiles describes only how the estimate moves as the seed varies on the sample in hand, and must not be reported as one for $\beta$.
\end{remark}
